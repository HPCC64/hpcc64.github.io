HPCC64 Lab

From reliable systems to better-informed decisions.

HPCC64 Lab studies how complex systems work, how people make decisions with incomplete information, and how artificial intelligence (AI) can help without taking responsibility away from people.

This work brings together experience in enterprise technology and several developing research projects. They share questions, but each has its own history and must be judged on its own evidence.

01 / How the work developed

The questions widened over time.

HPCC64 did not begin as an AI laboratory. Its present direction took shape through several lines of work:

  • 2025 · Reliability comes first

    The early focus was on important information systems: what happens when they fail, how operations continue and what it takes to recover.

  • 2025–2026 · Look beyond the technology

    The questions broadened to people, processes, costs and dependencies. Before proposing a change, it became necessary to distinguish what a system is required to do from what it actually does.

  • 2026 · Compare choices, not just products

    Assessing possible changes led to a wider interest in value: which option is worthwhile when money, time, quality and risk all matter?

  • 2026 to today · Knowledge, decisions and AI collaboration

    In parallel, research explored how evidence can remain useful across tools and over time, and how people can work with AI while retaining judgment and control. These questions now meet in HPCC64 Lab.

02 / People and experience

Human responsibility, assisted by AI.

The lab is led by Alexander Nikolov (Alex), who holds a PhD in System Analysis, Control and Information Processing. His career spans more than 25 years in enterprise technology, including technical analysis and journalism, work at Hewlett-Packard, SAP and Hewlett Packard Enterprise, and technology assessment and advisory for startups.

Human lead

Alex sets the purpose, research questions and limits of delegated work. He makes consequential decisions and remains accountable for them.

AI assistance

AI helps investigate, organize evidence, compare ideas, write and test work within defined limits. Questions beyond those limits return to the human lead.

03 / Current research

Three connected fields of inquiry.

  • Reliable technology and operations

    How can an organization understand dependencies, prepare for failures and recover important systems?

  • Evidence, knowledge and decisions

    How can we keep track of what is known, what is assumed and why one choice was preferred over another?

  • People, organizations and AI

    How do incentives, rules and actual behavior affect results? What work can be delegated to AI while keeping decisions accountable?

Practical work also examines open standards and replaceable tools where they help reduce long-term dependence on any one provider.

04 / Methods in development

Different tools for different questions.

The following are research projects, not finished products or universally proven methods. Their possible connections are being explored rather than assumed.

  • Assess what is needed and what exists

    Technology assessment starts by clarifying requirements, examining how a system actually works and identifying the gaps that matter.

  • Risk-Guided Value Engineering

    An approach to comparing possible changes by the value they may create and their costs, time, quality and risks. Its technology-focused application is called Risk-Guided Technology Advisory.

  • From decisions to action

    PASGR-7 is the working name of a seven-stage method for considering choices under uncertainty. EXEC is the related work on putting a choice into practice, checking results and handling errors.

  • Knowledge Fabric

    An approach to preserving claims, sources, context and differing interpretations so an analysis can be checked or revisited later.

What does HPCC64 mean? Human — who is responsible; Purpose — what is worth achieving; Cognition — how evidence becomes understanding and decisions; Control — how actions and consequences are checked.

The “64” stands for six areas to examine — value, people, processes, knowledge, decisions and technology — considered through four criteria: money, time, quality and risk. It is a name for this framework, not an arithmetic equation.
