HPCC64 Lab

Human–machine symbiosis, made explicit.

A human sets purpose and remains accountable. AI extends the capacity to research, reason, build and verify. HPCC64 Lab develops this collaboration through work on complex technological, business and social systems.

The research question is practical: how can we turn incomplete evidence into useful decisions and controlled action without losing sight of uncertainty or human authority?

01 / Participants

Different responsibilities. One accountable direction.

Alex · human principal

Defines purpose, values, priorities and acceptable risk.

  • Sets constraints and the scope of delegated work.
  • Decides consequential commitments and publication.
  • Retains final authority and accountability.

AI · cognitive collaborator

Extends investigation and execution inside that scope.

  • Researches, analyzes, synthesizes, designs and implements.
  • Checks work and preserves evidence, uncertainty and counter-evidence.
  • Escalates when evidence, authority or execution conditions are insufficient.

Additional agents, models, tools and cloud environments provide temporary, bounded capabilities. They are not a permanent staff or an independent decision-making authority.

Alex’s personal experience informs the collaboration. It remains separate from the research, artifacts and results produced under HPCC64.

02 / Research

Study the system as it operates.

  • Resilient systems and technology decisions

    Requirements, architecture, dependencies, business continuity and lifecycle economics. What happens under degraded conditions, and what evidence supports recoverability?

  • Knowledge and human–AI collaboration

    Traceable claims, competing interpretations, reproducible decisions and bounded agency. How can useful work survive changes of model, tool or execution environment?

  • Social and business systems

    Actors, incentives, formal rules, actual mechanisms and feedback. Where do representations diverge from operational reality, and what does that change?

Open standards, portability and replaceable components are considered where they improve resilience and preserve practical options.

03 / Developing methods

Connect value, evidence, decisions and action.

These methods are under development. The descriptions below set out their intended roles; practical effectiveness needs to be assessed in each application.

  • RGVE · value across domains

    Risk-Guided Value Engineering starts with the value a system should create, for whom and under which constraints. Its scope is cross-domain. Risk-Guided Technology Advisory (RGTA) is its IT-specific application.

    MTQR makes Money, Time, Quality and Risk / Uncertainty explicit when comparing alternatives. It is an appraisal lens, not a universal numerical score.

  • Required state and current state

    In IT assessment, compliance establishes obligations and hard constraints. Technology due diligence examines architecture, dependencies, operating capability and evidence. Comparing the two exposes gaps and viable alternatives.

  • PASGR-7 and EXEC · decision to action

    PASGR-7 develops a reasoned choice under uncertainty. EXEC turns a selected direction into bounded, observable action with success/failure criteria, error handling, escalation and reassessment.

  • Knowledge Fabric · evidence across the loop

    A knowledge architecture for claims, sources, provenance, context, uncertainty and competing interpretations. Its purpose is to make reasoning traceable and reusable across decisions and tools.

Why “64”? Six system domains viewed through four decision criteria: Money, Time, Quality and Risk / Uncertainty.

The domains are Value & Outcomes; Actors & Capabilities; Processes & Operating Model; Evidence, Data & Knowledge; Cognition, Decisions & Automation; Technology, Infrastructure & Dependencies.

04 / Practical work

Small enough to check. Substantial enough to learn from.

We investigate problem classes such as business discovery, service and operating-model design, institutional navigation and complex socio-technical change. Useful work needs a clear question, explicit assumptions, an observable result and a reason to revisit the decision.

Book · in development

Value-led systems and mission-critical IT

A book project connects value-led reasoning, appraisal of alternatives and IT advisory with infrastructure architecture and worked cases. It asks when an architecture is justified and when a simpler alternative is sufficient.

The manuscript is unfinished and is not yet publicly available.
