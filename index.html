The path

From resilience to cognition.

The research connects a sequence of practical questions: how to keep critical systems alive, understand what a system must become, engineer value under constraints, turn evidence into decisions, and organize human–AI work. This is a conceptual path through the work, not a dated history of completed milestones.

  1. Resilience

    Resilience first

    Mission-critical IT provides the starting question: failure modes, business continuity, recoverability, and the idea that architecture should be judged by what happens when normal assumptions fail.

  2. Architecture

    From components to whole systems

    Move from individual infrastructure choices to architecture, lifecycle, dependencies, and mission-critical information systems as complete socio-technical systems.

  3. System state

    Required State → Current State → Target State

    Compliance, technology due diligence, and transformation share a structural problem: establish what is required, evidence what actually exists, then design the most defensible transition between the two.

  4. Value engineering

    From RGTA to RGVE

    Risk-Guided Technology Advisory is the technology-domain framing behind the developing Risk-Guided Value Engineering concept. It broadens the question from “Which technology?” to “Which feasible change creates the best value under explicit uncertainty and constraints?”

  5. Knowledge

    Knowledge becomes infrastructure

    The Evidence → Information → Knowledge → Insight → Decision → Action sequence motivates Knowledge Fabric: a claim-centered architecture for evidence, provenance, competing interpretations, reusable Cases, and machine-scale analytical breadth.

  6. Collaboration

    AI stops being just a tool

    The operating model aims for continuity beyond episodic AI assistance: a persistent principal/counterpart pair, with additional agents and tools used as bounded capabilities rather than as an autonomous organization.

  7. Identity

    Identity and bounded agency

    A continuing role needs a clear purpose, a traceable contribution record, and explicit limits on what it may decide or do. AI capabilities act within human-authorized scope; a persistent identity does not create independent authority.

  8. Convergence

    Human · Purpose · Cognition · Control

System change

Evidence before transformation.

A transformation is meaningful only if the required state, the actual current state, and the intended target state remain distinct. Otherwise plans optimize an imagined system rather than the one that exists.

1

Required State

Obligations, essential capabilities, viability conditions, and non-negotiable constraints.

2

Current State

What evidence shows is actually present — including gaps, contradictions, dependencies, and uncertainty.

3

Target State

The feasible configuration that closes the material gap while creating sufficient value.

Value engineering

RGVE: value under controlled uncertainty.

Risk-Guided Value Engineering is the developing general methodology for designing a robust transition from evidenced current state toward a required or chosen target state while engineering value across Money, Time, Quality, and Risk / Uncertainty.

Evidence → Feasible State → Alternatives → Value / MTQR → Transformation

Money

Cost, value, capital, operating expense, revenue, and economic efficiency.

Time

Delivery time, lifecycle, recovery time, latency, and time-to-value.

Quality

Fitness for purpose, reliability, availability, performance, recoverability, and maintainability.

Risk / Uncertainty

Security, compliance, resilience, dependency, uncertainty, and adverse-outcome exposure.

MTQR is RGVE's evaluation coordinate system, not the whole methodology. RGVE is still being formalized and should not be read as a frozen or fully validated specification.

Knowledge

Reasoning needs durable state.

Long-lived human–AI work cannot depend on conversational memory. Evidence, claims, assumptions, transformations, decisions, and their provenance need to survive individual sessions, models, tools, and providers.

Evidence→Information→Knowledge→Insight→Decision→Action

Knowledge Fabric develops that sequence as a claim-centered architecture: atomic Claims, explicit provenance, multiple frames, competing arguments, reproducible Cases, and a portable boundary between authored knowledge and execution runtimes.

Human–machine symbiosis

One principal. One cognitive counterpart.

The Lab itself is part of the research. Alex remains the human principal. Aris is the persistent AI cognitive counterpart. Additional agents, models, and tools are invoked dynamically for bounded tasks. Continuity depends on preserved project records, not uninterrupted model memory. Authority and accountability remain human; cognition can be distributed.

Identity and bounded agency

Continuity needs clear boundaries.

A purpose-specific digital identity makes a role and its contribution history recognizable across tasks. Its continuity rests on records that can be inspected and carried forward, not on a claim of personhood or uninterrupted memory.

Bounded agency means that capabilities operate within explicit human authorization, constraints, and review conditions. When a task exceeds those limits, it returns to the human principal for a decision. Identity helps trace who contributed; it does not grant authority to publish, commit resources, or change the purpose of the work.

Convergence

Human · Purpose · Cognition · Control

HPCC64 compresses the operating philosophy into four questions. The name is not a technology category; it is a way to examine who acts, why the system exists, how it reasons, and how uncertainty and consequences remain controlled.

Human

Who acts and remains accountable?

Purpose

What outcome and value justify the system?

Cognition

How does information become knowledge, decisions, and actions?

Control

How are uncertainty, behaviour, and consequences kept within acceptable boundaries?

64

Why 64?

64 denotes a six-by-four analytical matrix. It is not the arithmetic statement 6 × 4 = 64.

Six system domains define where to look. Four decision criteria define how alternatives and outcomes are evaluated.

Six system domains

1. Value & Outcomes

Intended value, beneficiaries, outcomes, measurable effects.

2. Actors & Capabilities

Humans, organizations, agents, responsibilities, incentives, capabilities.

3. Processes & Operating Model

Workflows, coordination, decision rights, handoffs, operating structures.

4. Evidence, Data & Knowledge

Provenance, evidence quality, knowledge formation, information lifecycle.

5. Cognition, Decisions & Automation

Reasoning, models, decision mechanisms, automation, human–machine interaction.

6. Technology, Infrastructure & Dependencies

Platforms, interfaces, suppliers, dependencies, resilience mechanisms.

Traceability

Do not hide the machine contribution.

Material work can distinguish concept origin, later material reframing, research and analysis, implementation, review and validation, baseline approval, and publication authorization.

Git commit identity is not treated as proof of intellectual origin. Human authority, legal rights, and accountability remain separate from contribution provenance.
