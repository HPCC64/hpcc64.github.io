HPCC64 Lab · research trajectory

From resilient systems to human–AI collaboration.

HPCC64 Lab is an ongoing research and engineering program about how complex digital systems survive failure, how evidence becomes defensible decisions, and how humans and AI can work together without losing control, provenance, or accountability.

A human and an AI, working as one lab.

Evolution

The work did not begin with AI.

It grew from a long-running engineering question: how to keep mission-critical information systems useful when components, assumptions, organizations, or plans fail. The later methodologies are extensions of that same problem.

  1. 2003 → 2024

    Fault tolerance, mission-critical IT, and resilience

    The research roots are in fault tolerance for distributed systems, followed by two decades of work around enterprise and mission-critical infrastructure. Business continuity, availability, recoverability, data lifecycle, and hybrid architecture established the first principle: systems should be judged by behaviour under failure, not by the elegance of individual components.

  2. 2025 → H1 2026

    From infrastructure to system state

    Technology due diligence, compliance, and transformation work made the next problem explicit: separate what a system must be, what evidence shows it is, and what feasible target state can close the gap. This became the Required State → Current State → Target State pattern.

  3. H1 → Aug 2026

    Risk-guided decision engineering

    Risk-Guided Technology Advisory (RGTA) connected state assessment to the choice between alternatives. Money, Time, Quality, and Risk / Uncertainty (MTQR) became the common coordinates for comparing value and trade-offs instead of optimizing one variable in isolation.

  4. May → Sep 2026

    Knowledge becomes infrastructure

    Early Knowledge-as-Code experiments evolved into Knowledge Fabric: evidence, atomic claims, provenance, competing interpretations, reusable cases, and reproducible analytical state. The focus moved from storing documents to preserving the structure needed to reconstruct reasoning.

  5. Aug → Sep 2026

    AI becomes part of the operating model

    The Lab moved beyond episodic AI assistance toward a persistent human principal / AI cognitive counterpart model. Additional models and agents became temporary bounded capabilities rather than a simulated permanent staff; identity, authorization, failure handling, and provenance became first-class design concerns.

  6. Sep → Oct 2026

    Convergence into HPCC64

    RGTA broadened into Risk-Guided Value Engineering (RGVE), PASGR-7 connected decision structure to execution, and the different research branches converged around one system view: Human · Purpose · Cognition · Control. HPCC64 became the compact frame for the whole system rather than another isolated methodology.

The system today

One chain from evidence to controlled action.

The current work is easier to understand as one layered system. Each method owns a different part of the chain; none is intended to be a complete answer by itself.

01

State & evidence

Compliance Assessment, Technology Due Diligence, and Required → Current → Target State establish obligations, facts, gaps, dependencies, and uncertainty before transformation begins.

02

Value & choice

RGVE generalizes the earlier technology-domain RGTA approach. MTQR provides Money · Time · Quality · Risk / Uncertainty as common decision coordinates.

03

Decision & execution

PASGR-7 + EXEC connects structured decision selection to bounded execution, observable outcomes, reassessment, and explicit failure handling.

04

Knowledge & reproducibility

Knowledge Fabric preserves claims, evidence, provenance, context, competing interpretations, and replayable analytical state so consequential reasoning does not disappear with a chat session, model change, or deleted program.

05

Human–AI operation

The Lab itself is a test environment for bounded agency, persistent role identity, ephemeral workers, explicit authorization, fault tolerance, and separation between cognitive topology and execution topology.

HPCC64

Human · Purpose · Cognition · Control.

This is the shortest expression of the system: who remains responsible, why the system exists, how it turns evidence into decisions, and how behaviour and consequences remain within acceptable boundaries.

Human + Purpose

Human authority and accountability remain explicit. Purpose provides the objective function: value, constraints, beneficiaries, and the outcomes worth pursuing.

Cognition + Control

Cognition covers evidence, knowledge, reasoning, decisions, and automation. Control covers uncertainty, permissions, dependencies, failure modes, and consequences.

64

Why 64?

64 is the mnemonic label for a six-by-four analytical frame: six places to look at a system, evaluated through four recurring decision criteria.

6 system domains

  1. Value & Outcomes
  2. Actors & Capabilities
  3. Processes & Operating Model
  4. Evidence, Data & Knowledge
  5. Cognition, Decisions & Automation
  6. Technology, Infrastructure & Dependencies

4 decision criteria

  1. Money
  2. Time
  3. Quality
  4. Risk / Uncertainty
The Lab

A persistent partnership, not a virtual staff.

HPCC64 Lab uses one stable human–AI cognitive pair. Other models, agents, runtimes, and tools are temporary capabilities introduced when a task needs parallelism, verification, isolation, permissions, or a specific execution environment.

Alex — human principal

Sets purpose, values, constraints, research direction, and consequential decisions. Retains authority and accountability.

Aris — AI cognitive counterpart

Extends research, analysis, synthesis, design, implementation, and verification within the authorized scope while preserving evidence, uncertainty, and provenance.

The objective is not autonomous AI. It is a fault-tolerant human–machine research system in which cognition can be distributed while authority, state, provenance, and recovery remain explicit.

Resilience began as an infrastructure problem. HPCC64 treats it as a property of the whole system — technology, knowledge, decisions, organizations, and the human–AI relationship itself.
