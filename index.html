HPCC64 Lab

HPCC64 Lab studies how digital systems can remain useful when technology fails, information is incomplete, or operating conditions change. The work connects resilient infrastructure, evidence-based decisions, and human–AI collaboration.

Our starting point is a practical question: what needs to change, why is that change justified, and how can it be carried out with acceptable cost, time, quality, and risk? Answering it requires looking at technology together with the people, processes, and business conditions around it.

A human and an AI, working as one lab.

Background and development

The work grew from fault tolerance and mission-critical infrastructure into a broader study of system change. These are broad periods of development; several strands progressed in parallel.

  1. Research roots

    Reliable infrastructure

    Research on distributed systems and subsequent enterprise IT work brought together availability, recovery, business continuity, and data lifecycle management. The recurring concern was how a system behaves when its normal operating assumptions fail.

  2. 2025–2026

    Assessment and change

    Compliance assessment and technology due diligence extended that concern to the whole organisation: establish what is required, determine what the evidence shows is present, and choose a feasible target state.

  3. 2026 · decisions

    Comparing alternatives

    Choosing a target state requires comparing trade-offs rather than selecting technology in isolation. This work brought cost, time, fitness for purpose, and uncertainty into a shared evaluation of possible changes.

  4. 2026 · knowledge

    Preserving the basis for decisions

    Knowledge-as-Code experiments and later Knowledge Fabric research addressed how to retain sources, claims, assumptions, and reasoning so that decisions can be examined and reused beyond a single conversation.

  5. 2026 · collaboration

    Organising human–AI work

    Using AI across research and implementation made continuity, permissions, verification, and recovery part of the design problem. Project records provide continuity; additional models and tools serve bounded tasks.

  6. Current work

    Connecting the strands

    HPCC64 brings these questions together: how to understand a system, select a justified change, preserve the evidence, and carry out the work under human responsibility.

From assessment to action

The methods below address related parts of that problem. Assessment establishes the situation; decision methods compare possible responses; execution turns a selected response into observable work. Knowledge and human oversight support the whole process.

Establish what needs to change

Required State describes obligations and essential capabilities. Current State describes what the evidence shows actually exists. Target State describes a feasible change that addresses the material gaps. Keeping them separate helps prevent a plan from being built on assumptions about a system that differ from reality.

Compare value and trade-offs

Risk-Guided Value Engineering (RGVE) is a developing methodology extending Risk-Guided Technology Advisory (RGTA) beyond technology choices. It compares feasible changes across Money, Time, Quality, and Risk / Uncertainty (MTQR). These criteria support the comparison; they are not the whole method. RGVE remains under formalisation and validation.

Choose a course of action and carry it out

PASGR-7 is a developing seven-step decision method. Its original mnemonic is Problem → Architecture → Scenarios → Grading → Recombination → Review → Run: frame the problem, map the system, consider alternatives and scenarios, assess trade-offs, assemble a coherent solution, check its robustness, and act with feedback. This explains the historical name, rather than declaring a new fixed version of the method.

EXEC — Controlled Execution System provides the execution layer: assign responsibility, define the next action and its inputs, set deadlines and limits, check the observable result, and decide whether to continue, revise, or stop. PASGR-7 addresses what to do and why; EXEC addresses how to carry it out and verify what happened. Together they are intended to reduce repeated replanning and the gap between a decision and its implementation.

Keep decisions open to examination

Knowledge Fabric is a developing architecture for linking claims to evidence, context, alternative interpretations, and their sources. The aim is to retain enough analytical state to examine how a conclusion was reached when a conversation ends or a tool changes. Its architecture is documented; implementation and validation continue.

Keep execution within an agreed scope

AI-assisted work needs explicit permissions, review conditions, and ways to handle errors. The lab studies these requirements in its own work, with a clear separation between proposing an action, authorising it, and verifying its result.

How the lab works

The lab combines human judgement with AI-supported research and implementation. Continuity depends on maintained project records, rather than uninterrupted model memory.

Alex — human principal

Sets purpose, values, constraints, and research direction. Makes consequential decisions and retains authority and accountability.

Aris — AI cognitive counterpart

Supports research, analysis, synthesis, design, and implementation within the authorised scope. Additional models and tools are used when a task needs a specific capability or independent check.

Records distinguish contributions, evidence, review, and permission to act. This makes it possible to check work and recover from errors without treating an AI-generated result as an authorisation.

What HPCC64 means

Human · Purpose · Cognition · Control summarises the questions that connect the work: who is responsible, what the system is for, how decisions are formed, and how actions and consequences are controlled.

Human and purpose

Identify who makes decisions and remains accountable, whose needs matter, and which outcomes and constraints define useful work.

Cognition and control

Examine how evidence becomes a decision, how that decision becomes action, and what checks, limits, and recovery mechanisms the system needs.

Why 64?

The digits refer to six system domains and four decision criteria. The domains help structure the examination of a system; the criteria support comparison of changes and outcomes.

Six system domains

  1. Value & Outcomes
  2. Actors & Capabilities
  3. Processes & Operating Model
  4. Evidence, Data & Knowledge
  5. Cognition, Decisions & Automation
  6. Technology, Infrastructure & Dependencies

Four decision criteria

  1. Money
  2. Time
  3. Quality
  4. Risk / Uncertainty

Problems we study in practice

Practical research applies these ideas to complex social and business systems. The questions concern how organisations coordinate work, use limited resources, and adapt when information or operating conditions change.

Testing demand before committing resources

What evidence is sufficient to justify an investment? We examine how to test demand, compare costs and potential value, and stage commitments so that weak assumptions can be corrected before they become expensive.

Designing a service that can operate reliably

A service must connect customer needs with capacity, suppliers, costs, and day-to-day work. Research examines how to design those relationships, identify likely failures, and assess whether delivery remains viable when conditions change.

Coordinating decisions across organisational boundaries

Complex work depends on different participants, responsibilities, and information flows. We study how these dependencies affect practical decisions and how clearer evidence, handoffs, and feedback can support coordination.

Work produced so far

The work has produced manuscripts, architecture documents, and method descriptions at different stages of development. These are distinguished from finished products or demonstrated operational capabilities.

Hybrid Multicloud Infrastructure for Mission-Critical IT

A working book manuscript and planning framework on resilient infrastructure, business continuity, data governance, confidential computing, and hybrid multi-cloud architecture.

Knowledge Fabric

Semantic architecture and research documents covering claims, evidence, provenance, and reusable analytical cases. Implementation and validation remain ongoing.

Risk-Guided Value Engineering

A developing concept description connecting assessment and feasible change with the four decision criteria. It is not yet a complete validated specification.

HPCC64 framework

The human–AI system view and the six-domain, four-criteria frame described above, used to organise further research.

Across these activities, the aim is to connect a clear purpose with evidence, a feasible decision, and controlled implementation. Practical results then provide the basis for revising both the decision and the method used to reach it.
