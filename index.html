HPCC64 Lab

Research and engineering for complex systems.

HPCC64 is a human–AI research and engineering lab focused on complex technological, business, and social systems. We study how to understand systems as they actually operate, make robust decisions under uncertainty, preserve the evidence behind those decisions, and turn them into controlled action.

The lab combines human judgement with machine-scale research, analysis, synthesis, and execution support. The objective is not autonomous AI; it is a resilient decision system in which authority, evidence, uncertainty, and accountability remain explicit.

HPCC64 = Human · Purpose · Cognition · Control. The “64” is a compact six-by-four analytical frame: six system domains evaluated through Money, Time, Quality, and Risk / Uncertainty.

Human–machine symbiosis

HPCC64 uses a stable principal–agent model. The human and the AI perform different functions; the boundary between them is intentional.

Human principal

  • defines purpose, values, priorities, and hard constraints;
  • decides which outcomes are worth pursuing and which risks are acceptable;
  • authorizes consequential actions, commitments, and publication;
  • resolves value conflicts and decisions outside the AI mandate;
  • retains final accountability.

AI cognitive agent

  • collects and structures evidence;
  • decomposes problems and analyzes alternatives, dependencies, and scenarios;
  • synthesizes knowledge and proposes decisions or designs;
  • implements and verifies work inside the authorized scope;
  • preserves provenance, uncertainty, and material counter-evidence;
  • escalates when evidence, authority, or execution conditions are insufficient.

Additional models, agents, tools, and execution environments are temporary bounded capabilities. They do not form a permanent virtual staff and do not independently redefine the objective or authority model.

Research directions

Our interests meet at one question: how can a complex system create value and remain resilient when information is incomplete, actors have different incentives, and failures are normal rather than exceptional?

Resilient digital systems

Mission-critical infrastructure, business continuity, recoverability, data lifecycle, hybrid architectures, dependencies, and failure modes. The purpose is to design systems for degraded conditions, not only for the happy path.

Technology governance and transformation

Compliance, technology due diligence, architecture, lifecycle economics, and transformation decisions. The focus is the gap between what a system is required to do, what evidence shows it actually does, and what a feasible target state should be.

Knowledge and decision systems

Evidence, claims, provenance, competing interpretations, reproducibility, and decision state. Machine-scale analysis is useful only when conclusions can be traced, challenged, and reconstructed.

Human–AI systems and bounded agency

Persistent cognitive collaboration, temporary agents, permissions, observability, error handling, and recovery. The research target is useful autonomy inside explicit human control, not unconstrained automation.

Complex social and business systems

Actors, incentives, formal rules, actual mechanisms, power, friction, feedback loops, and path dependence. We are particularly interested in systems where declared structure and operational reality diverge.

Open and portable technology

Open standards, replaceable components, interoperable data, local and cloud execution, and vendor-independent boundaries where practical. Portability is treated as a resilience property rather than an ideology.

Methodologies and how they fit together

The methodologies are not independent brands. They form a decision-to-action chain, with evidence and knowledge management spanning the whole process.

Required State → Current State → Gap & alternatives → Value / decision → Bounded execution → Evidence → Reassessment
  1. IT Compliance Assessment

    Establishes the Required State: obligations, mandatory controls, essential capabilities, and non-negotiable constraints. It answers what must be true before a solution can be considered acceptable.

  2. Technology Due Diligence

    Establishes the evidenced Current State: architecture, dependencies, lifecycle condition, operational capability, economics, gaps, uncertainty, and material technical or organizational risks.

  3. RGTA / RGVE + MTQR

    Risk-Guided Technology Advisory (RGTA) is the technology-domain predecessor of the broader, developing Risk-Guided Value Engineering (RGVE) method. RGVE compares feasible changes and target states through MTQR — Money, Time, Quality, and Risk / Uncertainty, so one attractive dimension cannot silently dominate the decision.

  4. PASGR-7

    The decision mechanism for uncertain, multi-actor problems. It decomposes the objective and system, exposes assumptions, maps actors and dependencies, generates materially different alternatives, tests scenarios, compares trade-offs, and synthesizes a robust decision.

    PASGR-7 is a retained project name; no new acronym expansion is currently adopted. Its historically recovered seven-stage formulation is: Problem → Architecture → Scenarios → Grading → Recombination → Review → Run.
  5. EXEC

    The execution layer paired with PASGR-7. Once a direction is selected, EXEC converts it into bounded, observable action with explicit conditions, telemetry, success/failure logic, error handling, escalation, and reassessment. PASGR-7 answers what is rational to do and why; EXEC answers how to act without losing control of the decision.

  6. Knowledge Fabric

    The evidence and reproducibility layer across the entire loop. It preserves claims, sources, provenance, context, uncertainty, competing arguments, and reusable decision state so analysis can survive individual sessions, models, tools, and execution environments.

HPCC64 provides the common system frame around these methods. Its six domains are Value & Outcomes; Actors & Capabilities; Processes & Operating Model; Evidence, Data & Knowledge; Cognition, Decisions & Automation; and Technology, Infrastructure & Dependencies. Alternatives are evaluated through the four MTQR criteria.

Practical application

We use these methods in real social and business problems rather than treating them as abstract frameworks. The specific projects may change; the recurring problem classes are more important.

Business discovery and staged investment

Testing demand, economics, suppliers, channels, locations, operating constraints, and minimum viable commitments before irreversible capital is deployed.

Service and operating-model design

Designing services around customer value, capacity, quality, economics, dependencies, exception handling, and the realities of daily execution rather than a presentation-layer process diagram.

Institutional and regulatory navigation

Working through changing rules, multiple jurisdictions, formal procedures, incomplete information, and the difference between nominal requirements and actual operational pathways.

Complex socio-technical change

Analyzing systems where technology, organizations, incentives, human behaviour, and external actors interact — especially where local optimization can create system-level fragility.
